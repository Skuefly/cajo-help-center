Terra 116 Power Plates: thru-hull wiring

The Terra 116 has three factory-installed Power Plates — bow, cockpit, and stern — made to be drilled through when installing a thru-hull wiring kit, so you never drill the hull itself.

The Terra 116 has three Power Plates built in from the factory — one at the bow, one in the cockpit, and one at the stern. They're dedicated drill-through points for running wiring through the hull: motor power, fish-finder cable, Power-Pole runs, and auxiliary 12V.

How Power Plates work

Drilling directly into a kayak hull creates a leak point you have to seal carefully and re-seal periodically. Power Plates exist so you never have to do that. When you're ready to rig, you drill through the plate — not the hull — and install a thru-hull wiring kit into the plate.

Watertight wiring is a two-part system:

  1. Power Plate — the factory drill-through point (bow, cockpit, or stern)
  2. Thru-hull wiring kit — installs into the plate and seals the cable run

What owners run through them

  • Motor power leads (10 AWG or 8 AWG positive and negative runs from a battery in the cockpit to a bow or stern trolling motor)
  • Fish-finder cable (transducer cable, NMEA 2000 backbone, network runs)
  • Power-Pole power and control cable (if you're rigging a Power-Pole Micro Anchor on the stern)
  • Auxiliary 12V runs for lights, USB chargers, etc.

Which plate for what

  • Bow plate — bow trolling motor power, fish-finder cable to a forward-mounted head unit
  • Cockpit plate — battery-side runs and accessory wiring from the cockpit
  • Stern plate — stern trolling motor power, Power-Pole runs, stern light wiring

Use as many as your rig needs — bow motor wiring through the bow plate and Power-Pole wiring through the stern plate is a common setup.

The Outpost 100 and Outpost 128 carry the same three Power Plates.
