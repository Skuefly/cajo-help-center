Can you hunt from a Cajo kayak?

Yes — waterfowl, bow, and even deer. Go with a rigid hull over an inflatable Highlander; the Terra 116's open deck is the standout, the Outpost 128 carries the heaviest rigs, and gun or bow mounts bolt to the gear tracks with no drilling.

Short answer: yes, and plenty of owners do. Waterfowl and duck hunting is the most common by a wide margin — a kayak gets you into skinny, quiet water a jon boat can't reach — but people bow hunt from them too, and we've had customers deer hunt from a Cajo. The boat gets you in silently, carries the gear, and doubles as your fishing platform the rest of the year.

Two things decide whether it works well: which hull you pick, and how you rig it.

Pick a rigid hull, not an inflatable

For hunting, go with one of the rigid boats — the Terra 116 or an Outpost — rather than an inflatable Highlander. A rigid hull gives you a stiffer, more predictable platform to shoulder a gun or draw a bow from, a solid standing deck, and hard mounting points that take a gun rest or blind hardware without fuss. The Highlanders are terrific travel-and-stash boats, but a hunting setup wants the rigidity and the fixed rigging of a hard hull.

The best Cajo hulls for hunting

Terra 116 — the standout. Its large, open SUP-style deck is the reason. There's room to move, to stack decoys or gear, to bring a dog, and to set up for a deer rig without everything piled on top of itself. If you're picking one boat for hunting, this is the lead.

Outpost 128 — the heavy-rigging pick. It has the highest freeboard in the rigid lineup (8–9"), which keeps you drier in the chop and wind you tend to get on open-water waterfowl mornings, and 500 lb of usable capacity for a fully-loaded blind setup — layout gear, a dog, decoys, and a motor. When the rig gets heavy or the water gets big, this is the boat.

Outpost 100 — tight-water option. The smallest rigid (10', 400 lb). It doesn't have the open deck of the Terra or the capacity of the 128, but for a lighter hunter slipping into narrow marsh and flooded timber it's an easy boat to handle solo.

Not sure which fits your build? Start with how to choose your first Cajo kayak.

Rigging a gun or bow

You mount a gun rest or bow holder the same way you'd mount any accessory: to the boat's existing hardware. Railblaza makes a gun/rod-holder-style mount that fits the standard track interface, and mounts like that go onto your gear tracks or Quad Rails with track nuts — no drilling, no cutting, no altering the hull. Supported rigging like this is normal setup and keeps your warranty intact.

The one line not to cross: don't drill, cut, or otherwise modify the hull itself to mount hardware. Anything the boat already gives you — the gear tracks, the Quad Rails, the molded inserts, the D-rings — is fair game. See the accessory primer for what the track system takes.

Safety and regulations are your call

Hunting off a small boat adds its own rules on top of paddling ones:

  • Firearm and archery safety on the water is entirely your responsibility — muzzle discipline, a safe cased setup on the move, loading only when you're set.
  • Hunting regulations vary by state, season, and species — licensing, legal methods from a boat, whether the boat has to be anchored or under paddle, blind rules. Check your state and local regs before you go.
  • Wear your PFD, and remember that late-season waterfowl means cold water — dress for the swim, not the air temperature.

None of that is boat-specific, but a hunting trip is exactly the kind of day where it matters most.
