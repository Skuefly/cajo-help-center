Horizon seats: fit, positions and measurements

The Horizon Pro Seat has 8 positions (two base heights, four recline angles) on a 21" x 17" base, plus an optional Perch for a 9th, elevated position — and it doesn't swivel.

The Horizon Pro Seat gives you 8 real seating positions — two base heights (10" high, 6.4" recumbent-low) each across four recline angles — on a 21" x 17" base, plus an optional Horizon Perch (the Horizon Elevated Seat Perch) that adds a 9th position at 17.6" off the deck. It doesn't swivel, and both pieces also fit a handful of non-Cajo kayaks.

Most kayak seats give 6–12" of slide; the Cajo track is built wider on purpose. A 4'6" angler runs the seat all the way forward and still reaches the pedals with the Traverse Drive; a 6' angler sits about mid-track; a 7' angler slides all the way back for full leg extension. Tournament anglers running heavy electronics or a sit-stand rig often slide slightly forward of neutral to get the standing platform under them when they get up. The whole seat clips in and out of the track in seconds, no tools — most owners pull it for transport and reseat at the put-in, and the track returns you to the same spot every trip.

Why it doesn't swivel

Cajo skipped the swivel on purpose. A swivel mechanism is a wear point — saltwater, sand, and grit get into the bearings and it breaks down over time — and it adds rotational weight and mounting complexity to a seat that already carries big rigging loads. That engineering effort went instead into the 8 positions, the 24" slide, the wide 21" x 17" base, and tool-free clip-in/out. Most anglers don't end up using a swivel as much as they expect to.

Fishing behind you without one:

  • Hand-steer with the Traverse Rudder Handle, right at your hip, and pivot the boat under you
  • Pedal in reverse — the Traverse Pedal Drive has instant reverse, so you can spin the boat with the rudder while pedaling forward or back
  • Stand and turn — every Cajo has a solid standing deck, so you can pivot your body without rotating the seat

The Horizon Perch

The Horizon Perch — full name the Horizon Elevated Seat Perch — is a separate add-on that mounts to the back of the seat frame — fold the seat back down and sit on the Perch itself, and you're at 17.6" off the deck, about 4" above the seat base. That's enough height for sight-casting and spotting tailing fish in skinny water, or just a better vantage point on flat water. It's a popular add-on for flats-style fishing.

Pedaling from the Perch

You can pedal the Traverse Drive from the Perch — it works as a full pedaling position, not just an anchored or drift seat, once you add one piece of hardware: an extra Steering Handle Base mounted to the gear track on the Perch itself, so the handle comes up to you instead of staying at deck height. That one change turns the Perch from a sight-fishing throne into a real trolling station — better visibility on structure, weed lines, and bait; a better casting angle; and easier sit-stand transitions since the Perch is already closer to standing height. The Traverse Drive's 10:1 gear ratio keeps your leg cadence comfortable even trolling from up high.

This works on the Outpost 100, Outpost 128, and Terra 116 (rigid) and the Highlander 100 and Highlander 120 (inflatable); the Highlander 140T works too if it's rigged for the Perch, though long-haul testing on the tandem is still TBD. The extra Steering Handle Base is the same SKU across all of them.

Low position for surf and fast water

The recumbent-low setting drops the rear of the seat base to 6.4" (from the 10" high position) while the front support stays put, so your knees stay up and your feet still reach the pedals — you're not lying flat, just sitting low and slightly reclined with your weight pushed into the hull rather than perched on top of it. A lower center of gravity gives an incoming wave less leverage to flip the boat backward as it hits the bow, and the same logic helps in fast tidal currents, chop, or high-wind days. Most anglers run the 10" high position for general fishing and drop to recumbent-low only when conditions call for it — surf launches, surf landings as the swell picks up, fast currents, or battening down on windy days. The switch takes a few seconds and happens on the seat itself, without unclipping it from the track.

Fitting non-Cajo kayaks

The Horizon Perch adapts to the seat platform rather than the hull — same hardware spacing, same gear-track footprint — which is why one Perch works across Cajo rigid hulls, the AirTrek inflatables, and any kayak on the Vibe Hero 2.0 platform. The Horizon Pro Seat, by contrast, is built around Cajo and Vibe gear-track spacing specifically.

Telling whether your Yellowfin Tandem has the Vibe Hero 2.0 seat: it has a squared-off aluminum frame (not the older rounded tubing), it mounts to gear tracks rather than dropping into a recessed well, and anything from 2019 onward is almost certainly Hero 2.0. Older than that, you're more likely on an earlier Vibe platform the Horizon Perch won't mate to cleanly.

The Horizon Perch doesn't change the AirTrek's stability much either — the inflatable's wide, stiff drop-stitch floor absorbs the higher center of gravity better than a narrower rigid hull would; it's still a higher seat, it just feels stable getting there. On the Highlander, your three seat options are the stock seat that ships in the box, the Perch (a clean bolt-under-the-stock-seat upgrade), or a Pro Seat strapped to the D-rings if you already own one.
